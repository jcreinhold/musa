# Capture without a tempo

Some music is played before it is measured. Free capture records the take with no assumed pulse, and then lets you
supply the pulse as evidence in Review.

## Capture freely

Press **Capture** and play. No count-in is forced when the piece has no clock for the passage.

Press **Finish**, then **Review**. The proposal opens marked: the app will offer a pulse, a meter, and a phase, and it
says plainly that these are uncertain rather than presenting them as read facts.

## Tap the pulse

1. Press **Tap the pulse**.
2. Tap along with the take — the button counts your taps: *Read 4 taps*.
3. Press it again to read them.

Your taps constrain the same take and produce a new proposal. They are evidence about the performance, not an edit to
the piece: tapping never changes the piece's own `tempo` or `meter`.

Two taps are the minimum. One tap says where a beat was but nothing about how fast, and the app says so instead of
guessing from it.

## Mark a downbeat

Where the beat is clear but the bar is not, mark which of your taps is beat one. Without it a phrase can be read
correctly and still be barred a beat late, which is a different piece of music.

## When the answer must be a context change

If the phrase genuinely will not fit the piece's meter, the proposal says so rather than bending. A change of tempo or
meter is written and accepted explicitly, as source. The alternative — the app silently re-metering your piece so a
four-bar phrase can land — is exactly the class of edit Musa refuses to make on your behalf.

## Music that has no pulse at all

Choose the `unmeasured` policy for a passage that is genuinely ametric. It admits no beat subdivision and no tuplet, so
rather than gridding your playing it refuses to write measured notation and keeps the take for inspection. That refusal
is the honest answer: see [Capture and transcription](../reference/capture-and-transcription.md).
