# Written Pitch and Interval Arithmetic

## Task

Give Musa a total, spelling-preserving algebra for pitches and intervals, then expose musician-readable chromatic pitch
expressions such as `root up M2` and `pitch down m2`. Motif parameters must be able to determine every note honestly;
the algebra may produce arbitrarily altered spellings even when a particular surface or export backend later refuses
to engrave one.

## Read

- `docs/language/03-musical-domains.md` pitch and interval definitions.
- OMT `005-half-steps-whole-steps-and-accidentals.md` (accidentals and enharmonic equivalence),
  `006-american-standard-pitch-notation-aspn.md`, `016-intervals.md` (quality, compound, inversion, doubly/triply
  altered intervals), and `099-pitch-and-pitch-class.md`.
- Current `pitch.rs`, prompt 34's diatonic inversion repair, every external `WrittenPitch`/`Accidental` caller, and all
  four notation/MIDI backends.

## Design

Use the governing representation:

```text
Pitch    = (diatonic height, chromatic height) ∈ ℤ²
Interval = (diatonic displacement, chromatic displacement) ∈ ℤ²
natural(d) = 12 floor(d/7) + natural-semitone(d mod 7)
alteration(d,c) = c - natural(d)
```

Interval addition is componentwise and acts on pitch by translation. Supply the short proof: identity and
compatibility follow from the identity and associativity of addition in `ℤ²`; inverse is componentwise negation;
spelling preservation follows because the first coordinate chooses the letter while the second chooses sounding
semitone height. Property tests generate large positive/negative heights and alterations and compare with this reference
model.

`pitchclass` is the quotient by the subgroup generated by `(7,12)`, retaining spelling. Do not conflate it with MIDI or
with prompt 105's `pc12`. Parse named intervals into the pair, and print the conventional name when one exists without
making the finite name table the semantic domain.

Audit the current public pitch boundary before changing layout. Prefer extending its invariant behind constructors and
operations; if arbitrary alteration requires a public representation change, migrate all real callers in one prompt
and expose operations rather than fields. Backends either encode the spelling faithfully or emit a backend-specific
representability diagnostic. They never silently clamp, respell enharmonically, or change the semantic pitch.

## Target

- Pitch/interval expression syntax, formatting, highlighting, typed checking, and evaluation.
- Total internal/public written-pitch representation with checked constructors and no double-accidental semantic cap.
- `std::pitch`: interval constants, composition/inversion, pitch-class projection, and readable helpers implemented in
  Musa where they do not need representation access.
- Backend capability/diagnostic tests for at least natural, double, triple, and extreme alterations.
- `examples/pitch-arithmetic.musa`: the root-dependent turn plus compound and descending interval cases.
- `crates/musa-compiler/tests/pitch_action_laws.rs`: group/action laws, quotient law, enharmonic non-equality, round
  trips, and negative backend cases.

## Stop

- No ambient scale, degree stepping, `pc12`, tuning/frequency, MIDI-number ontology, or automatic enharmonic spelling.
- No fixed accidental enum presented as the mathematical domain.
- No theory claim based only on equal-tempered sound; D-sharp and E-flat remain different written values.
- Do not add a generic temperament trait; current callers need written twelve-semitone notation, not an imagined family.
