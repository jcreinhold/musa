# Candidate F₂ — keys are frames, not global coordinates

**Status: candidate component. Governs nothing.** The first fibred candidate in [04](04-candidate-fibred.md) indexed
timelines by meter. This note uses indexing for a different phenomenon: musical objects whose coordinates depend on a
chosen context. Pitch spelling, scale degree, tuning, chord naming, and harmonic analysis all have this character.

**One-line claim.** A context-sensitive musical world is a category of frames together with a family of process worlds
reindexed along frame changes. In a transpositional pitch model, a key is an orbit of a scale frame, a pitch collection
is a free commutative construction, and transposition acts functorially on it. Repertoires without keys need not supply
that frame theory.

This candidate is intentionally conditional. It should make keys natural where key is meaningful without making 12-TET,
octave equivalence, or tonic function part of the universal kernel.

---

## 1. What the examples require

The pitch quotient tower in [05](05-candidate-torsor.md) mixed several kinds of dependence.

- Enharmonic equivalence depends on a tuning or temperament.
- Scale degree depends on a scale or key frame.
- Chord name and harmonic function depend on an analytical grammar and local context.
- Pitch-class and set-class analysis deliberately forget register and sometimes spelling.
- Serial row operations use a different transformation system.

The common datum is not one quotient chain. It is **change of coordinates or context**. The correct abstraction should
make transport along a declared context change functorial while allowing different theories to have different context
categories.

---

## 2. Indexed worlds over frames

**Definition 15.1 (frame category).** A frame category `K` has musical contexts as objects and admissible changes of
context as maps. A frame-indexed process world is a pseudofunctor

`W : Kᵒᵖ → SymMonCat`.

For `k ∈ K`, the fiber `W(k)` contains objects and processes meaningful in context `k`. A map `u : k → ℓ` induces a
reindexing functor `u* : W(ℓ) → W(k)`, coherently with identities and composition.

This is the same mathematical shape as temporal indexing in [14](14-candidate-temporal-locality.md), but the bases and
consumers differ. A temporal map restricts an observation region. A frame map changes coordinates, tuning, analytical
assumptions, or repertoire. They should not be forced into one undifferentiated index language.

### 2.1 A minimal transpositional model

Let a group `G` act on a pitch set `P`. An element of `G` is an interval-like transformation; the action sends a pitch
to its transposition. Freeness and transitivity may be assumed for a torsor model, but neither is required for the orbit
constructions below.

A finite pitch collection with doubling is an element of the free commutative monoid `M(P)` of finite multisets on `P`.
The action of `G` on `P` extends componentwise to `M(P)`.

**Proposition 15.2 (transposition extends uniquely to pitch collections).** Every action of `G` on `P` induces an action
of `G` by commutative-monoid automorphisms on `M(P)`. It is the unique such action for which the singleton map
`η : P → M(P)` is equivariant.

**Proof.** For `g ∈ G`, define `M(g)` on a finite multiset by applying `g` to every entry with multiplicity preserved.
Then `M(g)` preserves the empty multiset and multiset union, so it is a commutative-monoid homomorphism. The equations
`M(1) = id` and `M(gh) = M(g)M(h)` follow entrywise from the action laws on `P`; hence these homomorphisms form a
`G`-action. Each `M(g)` is invertible with inverse `M(g⁻¹)`.

For uniqueness, every multiset is a finite sum of singleton multisets. A monoid homomorphism agreeing with `g` on
singletons is therefore forced to apply `g` to every summand, which is `M(g)`. ∎

**JUDGED.** The construction underlying an unordered pitch collection or chord-class now falls out from the free
commutative monoid, while transposition of it falls out from functoriality. A voiced chord is finer data: a finite
family indexed by voices, registers, or parts. It maps to `M(P)` by forgetting that indexing; the quotient is not the
carrier of every chord.

## 3. Keys as orbits of frames

Choose a scale or collection template `S ∈ M(P)`. The group acts on templates by Proposition 15.2.

**Definition 15.3 (key family generated by a template).** The transpositional key family of `S` is its orbit

`Orb(S) = {g·S | g ∈ G}`.

The stabilizer is `Stab(S) = {g ∈ G | g·S = S}`.

**Proposition 15.4 (keys of one template are a homogeneous space).** There is a canonical bijection

`G / Stab(S) ≅ Orb(S)`

from left cosets to transposed frames, sending `g Stab(S)` to `g·S`.

**Proof.** If `g Stab(S) = h Stab(S)`, then `h⁻¹g ∈ Stab(S)`, so `g·S = h·((h⁻¹g)·S) = h·S`; hence the map is well
defined. It is surjective by the definition of `Orb(S)`. If `g·S = h·S`, then `(h⁻¹g)·S = S`, so `h⁻¹g ∈ Stab(S)` and
the two cosets are equal. Thus it is injective. ∎

This is the correct limited sense in which a key can “fall out” of a transpositional theory. The template `S` contains
more than a tonic, and the quotient is by its actual stabilizer. Major, harmonic minor, a maqam fragment, or another
collection may have different template data and transformation groups.

It does **not** follow that harmonic function is translation by a tonic. Function requires a grammar of expectations,
voice-leading, syntax, or prolongation over framed pitch material. Candidate F₂ provides the coordinate context in which
such a grammar may be stated; it does not derive that grammar from the group action.

### 3.1 Degree and tuning as frame-relative maps

Within a frame `k`, a scale-degree map may be a partial coordinate operation

`degree_k : WrittenPitch ⇀ Degree(k)`,

and a tuning may be a relation or map

`tune_k : WrittenPitch ⇸ Frequency`.

Changing frames transports these through the reindexing structure when the relevant naturality law actually holds. An
enharmonic spelling can coincide under one `tune_k` and differ under another. No universal pitch-class quotient is
needed.

---

## 4. Frame morphisms, functors, and modules

Some changes of frame are deterministic functors: a transposition carries every pitch and chord construction to a new
frame. Others are many-valued correspondences: modulation analysis, enharmonic reinterpretation, or tuning a flexible
pitch in a harmonic context.

Candidate M supplies both levels:

- a frame-changing functor yields a represented module;
- a contextual reinterpretation can be a non-representable module;
- lax monoidal structure states when pitch collections transport compositionally.

This prevents “key change” from becoming one overloaded primitive. The caller decides whether it has an invertible
coordinate change, a partial analysis, or a performance choice.

---

## 5. Type-theoretic reading

A surface language may present families such as

```text
Pitch(k)        Degree(k)       Chord(k)       Analysis(k)
```

but the kernel need not implement full dependent type theory. It needs:

1. frame objects and typed frame maps;
2. sort families indexed by normalized frame expressions;
3. explicit reindexing along frame maps;
4. a decision procedure for equality of the frame expressions used in types.

Musical claims about membership, function, or modulation remain propositions or module witnesses. They do not enter
definitional equality. This keeps type conversion independent of analytical theorem proving.

---

## 6. Normalization

There are three separate normal forms.

- Finite multisets have a canonical sorted representation once pitch atoms have a chosen decidable order for storage.
- Finite group actions or declared transformation words may normalize using a presentation-specific algorithm.
- Frame-indexed process diagrams normalize structurally as in Candidate S after their frame types have been checked.

Orbit equality is not automatically type equality. For an infinite or complicated transformation group, deciding whether
two frames lie in the same orbit may be difficult. The kernel may retain an explicit transformation witness instead of
solving the orbit problem.

---

## 7. Examples outside common-practice tonality

- **Modal collections:** choose a collection template and a transformation group appropriate to the mode theory; do not
  infer major/minor function.
- **Just intonation:** use interval ratios acting on frequency-relative pitches; spelling-to-frequency remains context
  dependent.
- **Microtonal equal temperaments:** choose `G = ℤ/nℤ` only in that package, not in the kernel.
- **Spectral organization:** frames may be partials or frequency fields rather than scale degrees; a group action may be
  the wrong local model, in which case use a more general frame category.
- **Serial music:** row forms and their transformation group form a frame theory distinct from key.
- **Non-keyed traditions:** supply pitch and transformation contexts without instantiating Definition 15.3 at all.

The general frame category is deliberately broader than the group-action example. If every useful example nevertheless
requires an unrelated custom category with no shared reindexing laws, this candidate has become a naming convention and
should be rejected.

## 8. Emergence audit

| Intended concept | Does it fall out? | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finite pitch collection | **Yes, in the simple model** | free commutative monoid |
| Transposition of collections | **Yes** | Proposition 15.2 |
| Key family for a scale template | **Yes, conditionally** | orbit/homogeneous space, Proposition 15.4 |
| Scale degree | **Partly** | coordinate map in a chosen frame |
| Enharmonic equivalence | **Contextual, correctly** | fiber-specific tuning relation |
| Harmonic function | **No, correctly** | requires grammar beyond frame action |
| Voicing and voice leading | **Not from multiset** | require finer indexed families and process maps |
| Universal non-Western pitch theory | **No** | framework permits native frame theories; it does not invent them |

**Verdict.** Candidate F₂ makes chord collections, transposition, and a precise limited notion of key arise from
standard universal constructions instead of kernel keywords. It also states the boundary honestly: harmonic syntax and
cultural meaning do not follow from symmetry alone. The remaining question is whether temporal regions and musical
frames can be combined with process worlds and modules in a small term calculus without reproducing full multimodal
dependent type theory.
