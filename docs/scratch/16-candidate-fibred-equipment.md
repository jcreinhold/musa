# Candidate E₂ — a fibred equipment of process worlds

**Status: semantic upper-bound candidate; not the current implementation recommendation. Governs nothing.** Candidates
[11](11-candidate-relational-presentation.md) through [15](15-candidate-frames.md) were kept separate to expose their
side conditions. This note asks whether their surviving parts have one small internal language.

**Current disposition.** [18](18-minimal-recommendation.md) observes that neither first-class worlds nor modules have
been forced by a Musa example. Prototype one multi-sorted compositional syntax first. Return to this candidate only if
the explicit escalation tests in 18 fail.

**One-line claim.** Musa's semantic house may be a collection of context-indexed symmetric monoidal process worlds, with
functors as native structure-preserving maps and modules as non-identifying links between worlds. A piece is a finite
typed diagram with witnesses on those links. The trusted syntax needs only typed maps, parallel composition, reindexing,
and link action/composition; full dependent type theory is not required.

In category-theoretic language this resembles a fibred symmetric monoidal virtual equipment. That phrase names the
semantic laws; it is not a proposal to expose higher-category jargon to Musa users or to reproduce the full general
definition in Rust.

---

## 1. Why each layer is present

The synthesis is acceptable only if every layer has a different caller.

| Layer | Exact object it retains | Constructor that falls out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native category | typed objects and processes in one world | processor chain; passage transformation |
| Symmetric tensor | independent parallel composition | processor bank; parallel tones; chord carrier |
| Context fibration | region/frame and change of context | excerpt; restriction; transposition/reframing |
| Module | witness relating objects in unlike worlds | many-valued realization; profile interpretation |
| Representability | module generated by a functor | deterministic realization functor |
| Lax monoidal module | compatibility of links with tensor | parallel realization of parallel construction |
| Finite presentation | generators, boundaries, and hidden intermediates | source artifact and structural normal form |

Deleting any row loses its named caller. Combining rows also loses information:

- a category alone forces cross-world realization to be a map;
- a relation alone forgets native transformations and naturality;
- a tensor alone has no region or frame in which “parallel” is interpreted;
- an index alone does not construct chords or processor graphs;
- a finite graph alone does not distinguish structural wiring from an arbitrary predicate network.

This is the best current argument that the layers are not decorative. It is not yet evidence that their combined term
system is minimal.

## 2. Semantic data

### 2.1 One world

**Definition 16.1 (context-indexed process world).** A world `w` consists of:

1. a base category `B_w` of contexts;
2. a pseudofunctor `C_w : B_wᵒᵖ → SymMonCat`;
3. optional domain equations or operations, stated inside the appropriate fibers.

An object of the fiber `C_w(b)` is meaningful in context `b`. A base map `u : b → c` induces strong monoidal reindexing
`u* : C_w(c) → C_w(b)`.

Temporal regions and pitch frames can both instantiate “context,” but they are not forced into one base theory. A world
which needs both may use a product, fibration, or another combined base only after its domain semantics justifies that
choice.

By the Grothendieck construction, the pseudofunctor can be represented by a total category `∫ C_w → B_w`. Its objects
are pairs `(b,A)` and its maps record both a base change and a fiber map. This observation matters for implementation:
the kernel can store explicit contexts and typed maps without implementing universes and arbitrary dependent terms.
Jacobs describes dependent types categorically through fibrations and the Grothendieck construction in
[first-order dependent type theory](../../../papers/logic-and-computation/type-theory/categorical-logic-and-type-theory/10-first-order-dependent-type-theory.md).

### 2.2 Between worlds

**Definition 16.2 (context-compatible realization module).** For worlds `w` and `v`, a realization module consists of a
module between their relevant total categories,

`P : (∫ C_w)ᵒᵖ × (∫ C_v) → Set`,

together with any required compatibility with projection to the context bases and, when parallel construction should be
preserved, a lax monoidal structure on the appropriate fibers.

The compatibility is intentionally not fixed more tightly here. A notation-to-performance link may cover a module
between notated and performed clocks; a pitch-spelling-to-frequency link may cover a tuning frame relation. Requiring a
single base functor would again force every relationship to be deterministic.

When `P` is represented by a context-preserving strong monoidal functor, the realization is deterministic and
compositional. Proposition 13.3 proves that composition of such represented modules agrees naturally with ordinary
functor composition.

### 2.3 A piece

**Definition 16.3 (piece presentation).** A piece presentation is a finite typed diagram containing:

- context objects and context maps in named worlds;
- native objects and process maps in the corresponding fibers;
- module names between worlds;
- link witnesses at selected pairs of native objects;
- an exposed boundary and hidden internal vertices.

The word “piece” here means a canonical Musa artifact, which may include a studio. A score-only work is a presentation
whose audio-facing data has been omitted or hidden. A particular performance or render is an interpretation or
completion of the presentation, not the universal definition of work identity.

## 3. Proposed dependently sorted kernel

The kernel has dependency only in its judgments: an object belongs to a world and context; a map's endpoints determine
where it may compose. The surface notation below is schematic.

```text
w world
b context @ w
u : b ⇒ c @ base(w)

A object @ (w,b)
f : A → B @ (w,b)

P : w ⇸ v
p : P[A,B]
```

If a native process changes context, use an object and map in the total category rather than pretending it lies in one
fiber. The first prototype may restrict native `f` to a fiber and make reindexing explicit.

### 3.1 Generic term constructors

The proposed structural constructors are:

```text
id_A
g ∘ f
f ⊗ g
swap_(A,B)

u* A
u* f

f ▷ p          left action of a native map on a link witness
p ◁ g          right action
p ⊗ q          parallel link witnesses, when the module is lax monoidal
hide_y(p, q)   compose through an intermediate object/witness
```

`hide_y` is presentation syntax for a multi-link cell or existentially hidden intermediate. It need not materialize a
coend object in the kernel. This is why a **virtual** equipment is the closer semantic guide: meaningful multiary link
composition can exist even when no chosen composite module is available.

There are no generic lambdas, applications, `Π`, `Σ`, identity proof terms, universes, recursion, effects, or stream
constructors in this layer. A user-facing functional language may elaborate pure computations and declarations into the
finite diagram.

### 3.2 Formation rules, stated rather than implied

Representative rules are:

```text
f : A → B @ (w,b)     g : B → C @ (w,b)
─────────────────────────────────────────
g ∘ f : A → C @ (w,b)

f : A → B @ (w,b)     g : C → D @ (w,b)
─────────────────────────────────────────
f ⊗ g : A ⊗ C → B ⊗ D @ (w,b)

u : b ⇒ c @ base(w)   f : A → B @ (w,c)
─────────────────────────────────────────
u*f : u*A → u*B @ (w,b)

f : A′ → A @ w        p : P[A,B]        g : B → B′ @ v
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
f ▷ p ◁ g : P[A′,B′]
```

The last rule suppresses context compatibility premises which must be explicit in a real calculus. Those premises are
the main unresolved design problem, not clerical detail.

## 4. Structural laws

The laws fall into four familiar groups.

1. Native maps satisfy category laws.
2. Tensor and symmetry satisfy symmetric monoidal coherence and interchange.
3. Reindexing preserves identities, composition, tensor, and symmetry up to the declared coherent comparison.
4. Link action is functorial on both sides; lax monoidal link comparison is natural and coherent; multi-link composition
   is associative and unital in the virtual sense.

No musical law appears in this list. There is no axiom saying voices have equal duration, keys have twelve pitch
classes, chords are tertian, meter is periodic, or a written mark has one numeric reading.

**Design obligation 16.4 (coherence).** The free structural syntax on a finite signature must admit a sound finite graph
presentation such that equality of structural terms is decidable, and every assignment of generators in a semantic model
extends to a structure-preserving interpretation unique up to the specified coherent isomorphism.

This obligation is the gate. If it requires users or domain authors to provide associator, unitor, pseudofunctor, and
module coherence proofs in ordinary use, Candidate E₂ fails the simplicity criterion. Those proofs must be guaranteed by
the kernel's free syntax and checked once in its metatheory.

## 5. Concrete normal form

The proposed normal form is a stratified typed hypergraph.

- **Context layer:** vertices and arrows in each world's base.
- **Native layer:** directed typed boxes and wires in fibers, with explicit reindex boxes where context changes.
- **Link layer:** witness hyperedges between native vertices in different worlds.
- **Boundary:** exposed context, native, and witness vertices; all others are hidden.

Normalization performs only structural operations:

1. normalize decidable atomic index expressions;
2. compose adjacent explicit reindexings and eliminate identity reindexings;
3. flatten native serial/parallel syntax into string diagrams;
4. apply symmetric monoidal coherence by canonical wiring;
5. flatten link composition while retaining hidden intermediate vertices;
6. alpha-normalize internal identifiers and sort independent components.

The result is canonical only modulo the chosen structural presentation. Domain equations—rational arithmetic, timeline
normalization, group-word reduction, DSP rewrites—remain owned by admitted deep modules. Semantic equivalence of
arbitrary audio or musical analyses is not conversion.

## 6. Denotational semantics

A model assigns actual base categories, indexed monoidal categories, modules, and module maps to the signature. The
interpretation of a finite piece diagram is obtained compositionally from:

- categorical composition for native serial wiring;
- tensor for native parallel wiring;
- reindexing functors for context change;
- module actions and multiary cells for links;
- hiding for internal choices or intermediate realizations.

Candidate R's regular logic remains one useful model: categories can be discrete, modules become set-valued relations,
and hiding becomes existential quantification. That model is no longer mistaken for the exact theory because it forgets
native arrows and their action on links.

Temporal sheaf or presheaf models instantiate the context fibration with observation regions. Audio models interpret
native maps as causal processors and tensor as parallel interfaces. Pitch-frame models instantiate reindexing with
transposition or contextual change. No one semantic category must identify their carriers.

---

## 7. Operational semantics

Execution uses an **orientation**, not a different denotation.

- Native maps in an executable world have implementations and schedules.
- A represented module inherits the implementation of its representing functor.
- A general module may have a solver, enumerator, probabilistic filter, interactive choice, or no execution strategy.
- Link composition passes witness/provenance data through hidden intermediates.
- Causal audio cycles require an explicit delay or guarded trace supplied by the audio world.

An execution request names target boundary objects. The compiler selects available orientations, builds a dependency
graph, reports unresolved link obligations, and evaluates finite maps or causal blocks as appropriate. Type checking
does not prove that a general module is executable.

## 8. What falls out, and from which definition

| Concept | Construction |
| --- | --- |
| Processor | native map in the audio world |
| Processor chain | native composition |
| Parallel processor graph | native tensor; mixers remain audio maps |
| Parallel tones / chord carrier | tensor of states in one temporal/frame fiber |
| Pitch collection | free commutative monoid in a pitch-frame theory |
| Transposition of collection | functorial extension of frame action, Proposition 15.2 |
| Conditional key family | orbit of a frame template, Proposition 15.4 |
| Excerpt | temporal reindexing/restriction |
| Unequal voice activity | different support inside one ambient fiber, Proposition 14.5 |
| Many-valued realization | module elements |
| Deterministic realization functor | represented module |
| Composition of deterministic realizations | Proposition 13.3 |
| Parallel realization | lax monoidal comparison, Corollary 13.5 |
| Notation/audio joint artifact | finite diagram containing both worlds and link witnesses |

Harmonic function, orchestral practice, meter theory, tuning, and acoustic primitives do not fall out from structure
alone. They are theories interpreted in the house. The test is that their *construction and transport* use the fixed
structural operations rather than adding new kernel forms.

## 9. Why this may still be the wrong kernel

1. **The semantic name may reflect real complexity.** If the context and module compatibility premises cannot be made
   local, the term checker is not small.
2. **String diagrams plus modules may be two calculi.** A single hypergraph representation has not yet proved that their
   normalizations compose cleanly.
3. **Temporal gluing is not native composition.** The synthesis retains both and has not shown the surface language can
   make the distinction obvious.
4. **Frame and temporal bases may interact dependently.** A tuning can change during a region; a tempo interpretation
   may depend on meter and phrase. Taking a product base may be too crude.
5. **Lax monoidality may be the wrong link law.** Some realizations preserve parallel construction only after global
   optimization, feedback, or resource sharing.
6. **Modules may be too proof-relevant.** Musa may need a proposition, score, probability, or cost rather than a set of
   witnesses; enrichment has not been selected.
7. **The free structural equality may be expensive.** Canonical graph equality and stable incremental IDs need an
   implementation experiment.
8. **Ordinary categories may be too weak or already too strong.** Homotopical equality is not evidenced; partial maps
   and nondeterminism may be representable more simply with restriction categories or relations.

**Current verdict.** Candidate E₂ is the first synthesis in the notebook for which chord construction, processor
composition, functors, and cross-world ambiguity occupy derived and visibly different positions. It is promising enough
to specify and falsify, not settled enough to govern code.

The next note runs it through a comparative sieve and states a smaller prototype language. No amendment to the current
kernel should precede that experiment.
