# Candidate T — the torsor and its quotient tower

**Source.** OMT `016-intervals.md`, `099-pitch-and-pitch-class.md`,
`101-pitch-class-sets-normal-order-and-transformations.md`, `102-set-class-and-prime-form.md`,
`109-naming-conventions-for-rows.md`, `072-neo-riemannian-triadic-progressions.md`.
`docs/scratch/kernel-hypothesis/01-atoms.md` Atoms 5 and 6 state this content as two atoms; this document argues they
are one thing and that the thing is the motive.

**One-line claim.** The realizations of a piece differ by *how much they forget about pitch*, the forgettings are
quotients by subgroups of one group acting on one torsor, and that tower is the part of the motive we can actually
exhibit today.

---

## 1. The observation from the residue table

[01](01-realizations-and-residue.md) E1 and E2: one note, six realizations, and each reads the pitch at a different
granularity — engraving needs the spelling, MIDI needs a number, set-class analysis needs neither. Nothing in E1 or E2
is contested; what is new is noticing that the granularities are **ordered**, and that each step down is a quotient by a
group.

## 2. The tower

`Pitch` is a torsor over the interval group: intervals are differences of pitches and pitches do not add (OMT
`016-intervals.md`). Write `G` for the full group generated by octave translation, chromatic transposition, inversion,
retrograde, and multiplication. Then:

| Level | Quotient by | Read by | OMT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| spelled pitch (line of fifths × octave) | — the finest level | engraving, voice leading | `006`, `016` |
| chromatic pitch | enharmonic identification | MIDI, sound | `099` |
| pitch class | octave translation | most analysis | `099` |
| set class | `T/I` | post-tonal set theory | `102` |
| row class | `T/I/R` (and `M`) | serial analysis | `109` |
| scale degree / function | translation by the tonic | functional harmony | `013`, `014` |

Two things about this table are worth stating carefully, because they are the whole candidate:

> **Proposition 5 (derived).** Each realization in [01](01-realizations-and-residue.md)'s table factors through the
> finest level, via the quotient map its own invariance names. Engraving factors through the identity; MIDI through
> enharmonic identification; set-class analysis through `T/I`.

> **Proposition 6 (cited, as a reading of OMT).** Normal order is a canonical form and prime form is an orbit
> representative. OMT `101` computes normal order by a deterministic procedure with stated tie-breaking, and `102`
> defines a set class as "a group of pitch-class sets related by transposition or inversion". Musical practice has been
> computing *canonicalize-then-quotient* for a century without naming the construction, which is precisely what
> `docs/scratch/kernel-hypothesis/01-atoms.md` §6 says.

This is the motive shape, and it is the one place in this directory where the Grothendieck comparison is doing work
rather than decorating: several realizations, each in its own coefficients, all factoring through one object, with the
realization maps being *forgetful* rather than arbitrary.

## 3. Why this is one atom and not two

`docs/scratch/kernel-hypothesis/01-atoms.md` separates the action (Atom 5) from the quotient (Atom 6). For a motive they
are inseparable: the group is what makes the levels of the tower *comparable*, and the quotients are what make the
levels *distinct*. An action with no quotient gives forty-eight row forms and no notion of row class; a quotient with no
action gives set classes with no way to say which transposition relates two members. The evidence log ranks both as
surviving untouched and cheapest to test — this document's only addition is that testing them separately would test the
wrong thing.

## 4. The morphisms, which is where this gets interesting and thin

Grothendieck's motives are not built by taking varieties and functions. They are built by replacing functions with
**correspondences** — many-valued, weighted relations — and then splitting idempotents.

[01](01-realizations-and-residue.md) E6 says musical relationships have exactly this character. "This passage is that
one transposed by four semitones" is a correspondence, not a function on notes; theme and variation, subject and answer,
model and sequence are all correspondences. And the projector idea has an obvious musical reading: **a motif is a piece
of the piece, cut out by an idempotent** — which is what `motif` in the surface language is trying to be.

Stated as sharply as this directory can manage today:

> **Conjecture (judged, not derived).** The right category for the motive has pieces as objects and `G`-correspondences
> as morphisms, and `motif` denotes a split idempotent.

This is the hottest live idea in the directory and it is also the least supported. It is written down to be attacked,
not believed. What would make it real: an account of what composition of two correspondences is, and a check that
idempotents actually split.

## 5. The hardest cases, run as the rules require

Not the easy ones. The test is whether "musically meaningful invariant = quotient by a subgroup" survives the cases most
likely to break it.

| Hard case | Is it a subgroup quotient? |
| --- | --- |
| Harmonic function (I, IV, V) | **Yes, surprisingly** — scale degree is the orbit under translation-by-the-tonic, so function is a quotient in the same tower. This one was expected to fail and did not. |
| Voice leading | **Yes, degenerately** — it needs the finest level and quotients by nothing. Consistent, but no evidence for T. |
| Neo-Riemannian `P`/`L`/`R` | **Yes** — the Tonnetz is the Cayley graph of the group they generate acting on the 24 consonant triads (OMT `072`; the group-theoretic reading is derived, not cited). |
| **Form** (AABA, sonata; OMT `086`–`088`) | **No.** Form is about *which passage repeats which*, which is correspondence data, not payload data. T says nothing about it. |
| **Timbre / orchestration** (OMT `114`–`115`) | **No.** Not a quotient of pitch by anything. Possibly not in the motive at all — see [01](01-realizations-and-residue.md) E8. |
| **Rhythm** | **No.** T is a candidate for the payload only. The base is [04](04-candidate-fibred.md)'s problem. |

Three of six fail. That is the honest scope: **T is a complete account of the pitch payload and no account of anything
else.** Two of the three failures (form, timbre) are the two the directory already flagged as hottest-iron in
[00](00-the-motive-question.md) §5, which is at least consistent.

## 6. Derivations

**D-1. The layer table of roadmap §2 falls out.** "Written pitch ≠ MIDI number" is the statement that two levels of the
tower are distinct, and "dynamic marking ≠ decibels" is the same shape one payload over. The roadmap states these as
five separate prohibitions; T derives them as one fact about a quotient tower. *What does the work:* quotient maps are
not injective.

**D-2. `Canonical::canonical_key` is the tower's bottom, and that is a bug in waiting.** The kernel's whole payload
contract is one canonical key. If a payload is a point in a torsor and consumers need several quotients of it, then a
single key can only serve one of them, and today it serves the finest. Every coarser consumer must re-derive its
quotient. *What does the work:* Proposition 5. This predicts that analysis code contains hand-rolled normalizations — a
checkable prediction and the cheapest next probe in the directory.

> **Withdrawn by probes P-1 and P-2** ([07-probe-log.md](07-probe-log.md)). D-2 is false in every form. The tower is not
> missing from the code — it is *already built*: `pitch.rs` names the octave and enharmonic quotients, and `scale.rs`
> carries the scale-degree level with sections in both directions (`Scale::class`, `Frame::pitch`), a quotient map
> (`Frame::locate`, whose doc comment independently calls its answer "the canonical representative"), and a test,
> `a_classs_degree_is_its_framed_degree_with_the_register_forgotten`, asserting that a square between two levels of this
> tower commutes.
>
> **This is a better outcome for T than the confirmation would have been, and a worse one for D-2's method.** T's
> content is that realizations are quotient maps in one tower; an unrelated author building the pitch layer arrived at
> sections, a quotient, a canonical representative, and a commutation law, in that vocabulary, without any of this
> apparatus. That is convergent evidence the *description* fits. It is not evidence the description is the motive, and
> §8's verdict is unchanged by it: T remains an account of the pitch payload and of nothing else. What D-2 actually
> tested was whether the repo had noticed the structure yet, which is a fact about the repo rather than about music.

**D-3. Enharmonic respelling gets its exact account.** E2's requirement — the harmonic analysis survives, the
voice-leading spelling does not — is "transport along the enharmonic quotient preserves exactly the invariants of that
subgroup." This is Atom 6's Structure Identity Principle and it is the one place a proof-relevant equality earns its
keep, as that atom's §6.1 already argues at length.

## 7. Five-example sieve

| # | Case | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | trivial: one pitch | passes |
| 2 | one operation: `T₄` applied to a set | passes — a group element acting |
| 3 | composition: `T₄` then `I₈` | passes — group composition; `109`'s 48 row forms are the orbit |
| 4 | dependency: a quotient-invariant claim moved across a respelling | passes, and is the case D-3 is about |
| 5 | hardest: two passages in AABA form where the second A is transposed | **fails** — T relates the payloads and has nothing to say about "is a repetition of". Form is correspondence data and §4's conjecture is exactly the missing piece. |

## 8. Verdict

**The leading candidate for the payload half of the motive, and the one to build first**, because it needs no kernel
change (the evidence log says so of both its atoms), because D-2 gives it a cheap falsifying probe, and because it is
the only candidate here that survived its hardest pitch case. It is not the whole motive: §5 shows it is silent on form,
timbre, and rhythm, and §4 shows the morphisms — the part that would cover form — are barely more than a slogan.
